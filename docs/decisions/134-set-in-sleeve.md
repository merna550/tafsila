# ADR-134 · 2026-10-18 · Set-in sleeve block

**Context:** Issue #134 (FR-PAT-05, FR-VAL-01, -02, -04, NFR-01). Plan: [`docs/plans/134-set-in-sleeve.md`](../plans/134-set-in-sleeve.md). Method: "Set-in sleeve, one piece" in [`drafting-method.md`](../engine/drafting-method.md). All choices are cheap to reverse (a constant or one function); the toiles decide the fit ones.

**Decision:**

1. **The cap height is solved, not the 2/3 armscye-depth reference.** `solveSleeveCapHeight` (bisection, `src/sleeve/sleeve-cap.ts`) finds the height whose cap is 25 mm longer than the bodice armhole (`resolved["bodice.armhole*Mm"]`, #128). The reference cap height (2/3 × (B / 8 + 100)) gives a cap 3–76 mm _shorter_ than the engine's armhole for 24 of the 25 matrix bodies (size M: −23 mm; only `broad-shoulders-xl` gets +17 mm), so it fails FR-VAL-02. The solved height is 165–240 mm (size M 179 mm against 146.7 mm in the reference). The other reference points (cap top, bicep half-widths, wrist points) match to 0.1 mm; the test asserts the underarm `y` equals the solved height. The reference fixture is not regenerated: it stays Aldrich's starting value, and the gap is a fit question for the toile. If the toile shows the cap too high, the likely cause is the bodice armhole length (#128, about 494 mm at size M), not the sleeve.
2. **Target ease is the middle of the range.** `SLEEVE_CAP_TARGET_EASE_MM = 25` = (10 + 40) / 2; FR-VAL-02 still enforces 10–40 mm. The cap is split at the shoulder point so each half carries ease in proportion to its armhole length (back ≈ 12 mm, front ≈ 13 mm at size M); each half pairs with its bodice armhole and declares that share as `intendedEaseMm`, so FR-VAL-01 checks the halves to ±2 mm. The cap stays symmetric about the grain line, so the shoulder point sits about 12 mm (along the cap) behind the cap top (the back armhole is shorter).
3. **Notches match from the underarm.** Front single notch 75 mm and back double notch 90/96 mm up from the underarm, on the cap and on the bodice armhole; no ease lies below them. The shoulder notch is at the start of the front cap edge and at the bodice front armhole tip (the shoulder seam). Because the bodice armhole runs shoulder → underarm, notch pairs gained an optional `alignFrom: "start" | "end"` (schema `Notch.pairsWith`): the source notch's edge end the distance is compared from; the partner is measured from the matching end (`reversed` flips it). Without it the rule behaves as before.
4. **One sleeve piece, two cap edges.** Edges in path order from the back underarm: `cap-back`, `cap-front`, `underarm-front`, `hem`, `underarm-back`; the two underarm halves pair with each other. Cut 2, mirrored, grain on x = 0. Edges cannot overlap (finishing), so `Piece.sleeveCap.edgeId` became `edgeIds` (an array, lengths summed by FR-VAL-02). No producer existed before this issue.
5. **Lengths.** `elbow` = Aldrich's elbow line (half the sleeve length + 30 mm), `three-quarter` = 0.78 × arm length, `long` = arm length. `cap` (+40 mm) and `short` (+70 mm) hang from the underarm line instead of a share of the arm length: the solved cap height varies too much between bodies (a 0.4 share left 7 matrix bodies with no underarm seam). A hem less than 30 mm below the underarm throws `SLEEVE_LENGTH_TOO_SHORT`; an armhole no cap height in 50–250 mm can fit throws `SLEEVE_CAP_UNSOLVABLE`. Both are input errors: the pipeline returns a bilingual `fail` result.
6. **The module adds the bodice notches.** `sleeve/set-in` (order 30) drafts the sleeve and returns the bodice pieces with the armhole notches added (outline unchanged), as the circle skirt replaces the skirt block's pieces. `sleeve.type: none` does not apply: no sleeve, no armhole notches, the armhole keeps its seam allowance for a facing or bias binding; the finishing instruction is printed by the sewing order (#146). Cuffs and other sleeve types are out of scope.
7. **`ENGINE_VERSION` 0.4.0 → 0.5.0:** designs with a set-in sleeve (the demo dress) now get a sleeve piece and notched armholes.

**Consequences:** Every matrix body drafts every length with all FR-VAL-01/-02/-04 rows passing. Merna checks the cap height on paper against her Aldrich copy (TC-ENG-134-02) and sews toiles in 3 sizes; `needs:sew-test` stays until they are logged in `docs/test-cases/engine/sew-tests.md`.
